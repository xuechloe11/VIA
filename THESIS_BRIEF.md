# VIA Short: Thesis and Evidence Brief

Tags: **[F]** filing / company call · **[C]** Bloomberg consensus · **[P]** public records / press · **[B]** Bleecker · **[M]** our model/estimate · **[V]** verify before using

## Core pitch (variant view)
The market has accepted that Via is a tech-enabled *services* company. It has not accepted the consequences:
- **Growth:** consensus treats network deals as proof of a scalable platform.
- **Profits:** consensus models a software-like profit path.

In reality:
- The growth engine is turning Via into a municipal bus contractor.
- The costs of delivering hours sit below gross profit, where consensus has them flat.

Short at ~7x FY27 gross profit / ~67x FY27 adj. EBITDA.

---

## THESIS 1: The growth engine turns Via into a bus contractor

**Consensus / bull:** Whole-network deals are "an inflection point" (mgmt) that proves the platform and lifts revenue per customer.
**Variant:** Network deals mean Via winning Transdev's contracts. They are larger and stickier, but ~96% services, with inherited union labor, grant funding and operator margins. The better Via executes, the more contractor-like it becomes.

**A. What network deals are**
- Rochester, MN: ~$77.8M / 64 mo (~$14.6M/yr), from Sept 2026. Via provides "drivers and management for city-owned buses," replacing Transdev; 5 bidders **[P]**
- Mobile, AL: $12.1M/yr from Oct 2025. "Management, operations, maintenance, customer service, safety"; replaced Transdev **[P]**
- Sioux Falls (2024) and Twin Cities, MI (Apr 2026): full network takeovers **[P]**
- Four 2026 wins, >$40M annual contract value (~$10M each) **[F]**
- Network book is ~$70M once launched, ~13% of run-rate revenue, and mgmt calls it "the core of the growth today and tomorrow" **[F][M]**
- CFO names "transit operators" as a competitor type **[F]**

**B. Why the bundle only wins in mid-sized cities**
- Large agencies buy software and operations separately:
  - LA Metro split its contract (Jul 2025): software to Spare, Via kept ops after a ~15% price cut **[B][P]**
  - Met Council (2024) and Baldwin County (2025) moved software to Spare **[B]**
  - Hillsborough and Ben Franklin Transit lost paratransit software bids **[B]**
- CEO, Q1'26: in Germany "we have not yet been able to crack it beyond the microtransit vertical … agencies still treating microtransit as in a silo" **[F]**
- Germany Q2'26 revenue $18.3M, **−12% YoY**, vs US **+35%** **[F]**
- Plano's committee ranked Spare #1; Via won after Spare withdrew **[P]**

**C. Contractor economics**
- **Labor sets the cost:**
  - "~50% of costs are drivers" (mgmt Q4'25) **[F]**
  - Rochester's union won a 20% base-pay raise (May 2026) that carries over to Via **[P]**
  - Mobile staff transfer with seniority and benefits **[P]**
- **Budget-bound pricing:** Mobile's redesign is "for the same annual budget" **[F]**
  - Rochester: state + federal grants cover **80%** of operating costs **[P]**
  - Via risk factor: "winning by bidding so low that our margins are impacted" **[F]**
- **Margins unproven:** asked whether network deals pressure GM, CFO: "some of them are accretive, some less. It really depends on the exact deal" (Q2'26) **[F]**
- **Mix moving to services:** tech-enabled services cost / revenue: 49.7% (FY24) → 51.5% (FY25) → 51–52% (1H26) **[F]**

**D. Via's software edge doesn't carry into buses** (Sioux Falls, National Transit Database 2023→2024, Via's first year) **[P]**
- **On-demand:** trips per hour **+51%**, cost per trip −32%. Via's software works here.
- **Buses (the core of network deals):**
  - Cost per hour **+8%**
  - Trips per hour −6%
  - Ridership −0.7% (the +30% surge came in 2023, *before* Via)
- **Total system:** cost **+9.4%** vs Via's claim of a "neutral budget" and "−25% cost per ride"
- Caveat: one city, one transition year; 2025 NTD pending

**Bull counter → response**
- *"Longer, stickier contracts improve revenue quality."* True, and concede it. Durability ≠ margin. Operator-quality revenue earns operator multiples.
- *"Via wins because of its software."* Sioux Falls shows the software advantage lands in on-demand, not buses. Plano's staff chose Via for "expert transit operator experience, not just software."

**Signpost:** gross margin as network deals launch (Q4'26–FY27). Consensus has it ~flat at 40–41%.

## THESIS 2: Consensus models the profit path like software

**Consensus:** Operating leverage takes adj. EBITDA from −1.3% (FY26E) to +4.4% (FY27E), en route to mgmt's 20–25% long-term target.
**Variant:** With gross margin stuck near 40%, reaching 20% EBITDA requires opex to fall from ~57% of revenue to ~20%. In a services business the costs of delivering hours don't stop at gross profit. Consensus has them shrinking.

**A. Gross margin isn't the lever**
- **Recent quarters:** GM 39.3% / 39.5% / 39.3% / 41.0% (Q3'25–Q2'26) **[F]**
- **Q2 step-up was one-time revenue:**
  - One-time revenue was 5% of Q2'26 revenue vs 2% in Q2'25 **[F]**
  - Mgmt expects GM to revert **[F]**
- **Consensus GM:** 40.0% (FY26E) → 40.8% (FY27E), no path to the 50% target **[C]**
- **Math to 50%:** at 80% / 25% GM for software / services, 50% blended needs services at ~55% of revenue (today ~75%) or services GM ~38% **[M]**

**B. Hour-driven costs sit below gross profit**
- 10-K: G&A "includes customer support costs … and insurance expenses" **[F]**
- 2025 insurance expense **+$6.8M** (auto liability and D&O) **[F]**
- Bleecker estimates insurance at 3–4 pts of GM **[B]**

**C. Consensus opex assumptions**
| | FY26E | FY27E | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $552M | $675M | **+22%** |
| Total opex | $303M | $315M | +3.9% |
| G&A ex-SBC | $83.8M | $83.3M | **−0.6%** |
| Adj. EBITDA | −$7.4M | $29.4M | −1.3% → 4.4% margin |

- A 1-pt miss on FY27 opex/revenue ≈ $6.7M ≈ ~23% of FY27 EBITDA **[C][M]**

**D. Growth costs money up front**
- Q3'26 guide: adj. EBITDA −$4.5M to −$3.5M, citing "deliberate investment in launching new network and school customers" **[F]**
- **Adj. EBITDA excludes stock comp:** SBC is ~$16M/qtr, and GAAP operating income is still −$7.3M in Q4'27E **[C]**

**Bull counter → response**
- *"Adj. opex +$10M since Q1'23 while quarterly revenue grew $74M"* (mgmt Q1'26) **[F]**
  - That leverage came from fixed R&D/S&M during the microtransit software era.
  - The next leg is bus operations, where support, insurance and launch staff scale with hours.

**Signpost:** FY27 opex guide. G&A growth vs revenue growth.

## SUPPORTING: Revenue is hours that governments control
Use inside the theses, risks and Q&A.
- **Growth mix:** "revenue expansion with existing customers drove the majority of our revenue growth" **[F]**
  - Organic customer growth only ~9%; 94 of 158 adds came from the Downtowner acquisition **[F]**
- **Pricing:** revenue is volume-based ("fleet size, minimum vehicles, or total vehicle-hours") and recognized as used **[F]**
- **Cuts show up as downsell, not churn:**
  - Jersey City roughly halved Via spend (~$4M/yr, Jul 2026) **[P]**
  - Arlington ceiling −31% **[B]**
- **Retention metrics miss downsell:**
  - Gross retention excludes downsell (S-1) **[F]**
  - Net retention (119%) disclosed once **[F]**
- **Committed revenue:** only **~50%** of H2'26 and **~17%** of FY27 consensus revenue is in RPO; government contracts carry "termination for convenience" **[F][M]**
- **Funding:**
  - Federal stopgap through Dec 11 **[P]**
  - 10-K flags paused IIJA/IRA disbursements **[F]**
  - ~Half of agencies expect severe budget problems through FY28 (APTA) **[P]**
  - ARPA funds must be spent by end-2026 **[V]**
- **Metrics look more recurring than they are:**
  - Run-rate revenue = quarter × 4, including one-time revenue **[F]**
  - "97% recurring" = variable hourly fees **[F]**
- **Pricing pressure:** 2026 paratransit at **$67.75/hr** vs $82.40 catalog midpoint (≈14% GM on Bleecker's cost stack) **[P][M]**

  - Today's price implies ~12x software GP if services get 3x

  - BBG classifies VIA as 100% "Application Software"

  - Blended forward P/E 96.6x vs 30.6x; re-rated from ~50x after the Q2 print **[P]**

## Catalysts
1. Q3'26 print (Nov): margin after one-time revenue; the H2-weighted launch ramp
2. FY27 guidance: opex and margin outlook
3. Addison / Highland Park 6-month pilot renewals (Oct–Nov 2026); Arlington renewal (Dec 2026)
4. Federal stopgap expiry (Dec 11); NTD 2025 release (Sioux Falls year 2)

## Risks → mitigants
- **Strong growth / beats** (US +35%, guide raised) → not a collapse call; thesis is margin and multiple
- **Network deals scale revenue per customer** → concede durability; the multiple should still be an operator's
- **AVs remove driver cost** → no timeline from mgmt; savings go to cities at re-procurement (Mobile: "same annual budget")
- **H2 launches bring one-time revenue** that props up GM again → don't make the Q3 margin a single-point catalyst
- **Squeeze:** short interest 9.9% of float, ~8 days to cover → size accordingly; cover triggers are GM rising with network mix, or opex guide below revenue growth with G&A flat
