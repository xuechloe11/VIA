Who sells to Carvana, who buys, and is supply really shrinking?

Built from Carvana and CarMax filings, Carvana's loan-level securitization data, a 10,000-person consumer panel, and Cox Automotive industry data.

Q3'26 results come out after the close today. Refresh the unit chart when they land.

Bottom line

  1. Sellers want speed and certainty. 54% of people who sold to Carvana say convenience was the main reason; 43% say highest price. Nothing locks them in. Carvana wins each sale on mindshare and on paying more for retail-grade cars.
  2. Sellers aren't leaving. Cars Carvana buys from customers and then wholesales grew 44% YoY in Q2'26 (about 45% TTM). CarMax's purchases from consumers were flat. Carvana is taking share of a flat pool.
  3. The supply constraint is mostly Carvana's own. In Carvana's own words, "supply" means reconditioning throughput. The macro shortage of late-model cars bottomed in 2025 and eases into 2027.
  4. Buyers are near-prime to prime and shop by monthly payment. Prime-pool FICO 702, 76% of loan balances on 73 to 84 month terms, average loan about $30K. Customers earning over $100K grew more than 60% YoY.
  5. Convenience is the main reason buyers choose Carvana, then price, then easier financing. Brand trust is rarely the primary driver and scores at parity with CarMax. The brand spreads by word of mouth.

Switching cost is near zero. Sellers pull two or three quotes, and third-party tests disagree on who pays most. Carvana keeps winning because it can pay more for retail-grade cars: it earns on each car three ways (retail GPU, loan sale, VSC/GAP; total GPU $7,014 in Q2'26), skips auction fees, and is the default first click. The ways sellers leak are offer cuts at inspection, title and payoff delays (the top BBB complaint theme), and CarMax pricing more aggressively in 2026.

On Hertz: Hertz is a Carvana supplier, not a competitor. It has sold ex-rental cars through Carvana as a "retail marketplace partner" since 2021. The real buy-side competitor is CarMax (68% of sales omnichannel, 13% fully online, investing in price in 2026). Franchise dealers and Amazon Autos (dealer-listed used cars in 130 cities) come next.

Prime buyers are held back by selection and by buying sight unseen. Subprime buyers are held back by down payment and loan terms. Selection is a production problem, so more reconditioning capacity unlocks the most profitable customers directly.

Tonight

What to watch in the Q3'26 print

  1. Wholesale units ÷ retail units. A reading below about 0.45 would be the first real sign of sellers leaking.
  2. Whether inventory caught up with sales, and any commentary on production.
  3. Any disclosure of the customer-sourced share or the marketplace-partner mix.
  4. Other GPU against interest rates (Morgan Stanley models −$100 QoQ), and the income and credit mix of buyers.

Caveats

  • Wholesale vehicle units are a proxy for customer buying. They miss customer cars that Carvana retails.
  • The Bespoke panel skews lower-income (55% earn under $50K). Its Carvana sub-samples are small (190 sellers, 519 buyers).
  • The ABS data covers the prime shelf only. Nonprime deals are private (144A).
  • The 78% financing penetration is our estimate.
